# Earliest Written Concept


**Soul Power 1 – Low Power Mode: Wayfinder** The first soul power, Wayfinder, passively identifies viable paths forward whenever one exists. It considers the environment, social interactions, tools, and indirect solutions, but it cannot act on them. It cannot detect or suggest the use of his second soul power.

Wayfinder frames its guidance as whether any path forward exists, given time and interaction. It can verify the truth of any proposed solution: if a person offers a false method, Wayfinder will reject it as unviable. Currently, the only valid external path is through Greed; any manipulation by Greed that contradicts a true path will be flagged as impossible. Actions that make Greed more likely to help my character, no matter how slight the shift, would likely be confirmed, if such an emotion is even possible for her.

**Soul Power 2 Blur** - Allows him to return home independently.


*Author's notes: Blur was actually made first, but I put Wayfinder first as it was "low power mode". I made a non-magical character from the real world for a fanfiction and dropped him into Re: Zero. Specifically, in Echidna's realm. During the course of the story, he discovered that he had a soul that had been stolen. Reclaiming it allowed him to gain the power to go back to his reality, which was the teleportation ability. In addition to letting him return home, it also allowed him to teleport in ways that didn't make a ton of sense. I soon came up with a complication where his memory was erased, and I added Wayfinder to help give him some way to not be tricked, which was based on Wayfinder from the Swords of Victory.*


# Earliest Refined Concept

**Core Rule**

* Body rejects all internal magic; cannot generate, channel, or be altered by it
* Experiences external magical effects physically, but cannot manipulate magic internally

**Low Power Mode — Wayfinder**

* Provides awareness of the best viable path, if one exists; does not execute it
* Considers:
   * Environment
   * Social interaction
   * Tools or indirect solutions
* No skills granted; does not force outcomes
* Concept: “Is there some way forward, given time and interaction?”

**High Power Mode — Blur / Commitment**

* Executes a single, all-or-nothing attempt along the lowest-exertion viable path, if one exists
  * Attempt is limited by what the body can physically manage without taking a single rest
* Activated by conscious intent plus physical motion (taking a step)
* Performs a single continuous traversal in extreme time compression. Memory of traversal persists
  * Traversal is limited to what the body could theoretically accomplish under ideal conditions
* No mid-action decisions: no talking or social interactions; no learning; no selecting new tools 
  * Tools already equipped or carried may be used
* Exertion scales with distance, gravity, and resistance
* Exceeding limits = blur fails, snapback, severe exhaustion
* Concept: “If I commit my body right now, does reality allow this traversal to resolve?”
* World Integrity: Obstacles, locks, and structures remain intact  
  * Blurring open a door doesn't physically open a door
  * When successful, it's akin to a teleportation spell

**High Power Mode — Activation & Execution Constraints**

* Requires deliberate perception and intent
* Cannot be reflexive or automatic
* Actions must complete once committed; no mid-action adjustment
* Reorientation required between uses; prevents chaining
* Effects limited to the body itself; cannot directly alter the world or other entities


*Author's Notes: I started widening the concept. Still no dominion, or Mutual Recognition, because I hadn't yet removed the soul concept. Honestly though, I never felt it was a good way to gain powers, since the initial concept was no magic. The goal with the newly named Blur was to nail down proper mechanics. I wanted a teleport that had intuitive rules that also behaved in ways that an outsider would find confusing. Wayfinder was expanded a bit but was still basically Wayfinder.*

*After this point, I set aside that fanfic and began focusing on the concept of a non-magical body that nonetheless can do something akin to magic. That quickly led to Mutual Recognition, and the concept took place fairly smoothly after that point. Eventually I realized I didn't just have a collection of powers, but a full-blown ontology, which made me want to develop it even further. Going so far as to add a glossary and even a separate terminology for 'resolving' universe-level magic (mostly focused on causality) and 'realizing' normal magic.*
