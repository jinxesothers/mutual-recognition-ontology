# mutual-recognition-ontology

This repository contains a fictional ontological specification for a narrative causality framework.

It exists to answer a single question:

**How could a person originating from a reality without a Magical Medium nevertheless participate in a world where magic exists?**

Moreover, I had already decided the shape of that participation. I made the original versions of Path, Blur, and Dominion (magically inert body) before anything else. I had no idea whether I could do it to my satisfaction until I tried, and it simply happened to work out.

This is an uncompromised passion project. Everything appears exactly as I intended, and nothing remains that was not deliberately chosen. Honestly, I'm hard-pressed to think of a single thing of any importance that I removed during the course of writing.

The framework defines how the World resolves non-magical Anomalies into its causality through Recognition and Choice. It prioritizes internal consistency and consequence over everything else.

There are no assumptions made. Every question must have an answer as to why it works the way it does, with no hand-waving by the narrative. It describes a closed answer to a specific ontological problem.

Although this framework is closed and uncompromised, it is intentionally adaptable in application. Its adaptability arises from hard limits rather than extensibility: by enforcing strict ontological constraints and invariant resolution rules, it remains coherent across a wide range of narrative contexts without requiring any modifications. 

As such, I have licensed this as free to use, but derivatives are not allowed unless they are already offered within the framework of my ontology. 

The primary specifications are contained in '01-framework.md' and '02-magically-inert-matter.md'. These are complementary but independently applicable frameworks. The full ontology should be read as a description of how outcomes are resolved, not as a catalog of abilities or options.


No contributions are expected or required.

## Inspiration and Divergence

The original inspiration for this project came from the fanfiction *Sleeping with the Girls* by AdmiralTigerclaw, which articulated what is commonly referred to as the “Medium Transfer Principle.” That principle framed non-magical status as genuine incompatibility rather than resistance, an approach that remains, in my experience, the most uncompromising and punishing depiction of non-magic in fiction.

In that work, incompatibility did not confer safety or narrative protection. The protagonist was repeatedly injured and brutalized. Survival depended primarily on his understanding of anime genre conventions rather than on his immunity. Non-magic occasionally prevented specific effects, but it did not prevent harm, and never functioned as a power fantasy.

In that implementation, however, the non-magical condition was explicitly temporary. The character was already undergoing conversion to magical compatibility within the narrative timeframe, and this progression was important to the story’s direction.

This framework takes the opposite stance. It assumes that incompatibility cannot be diluted through gradual integration. The problem, as treated here, is not how long non-magic lasts, but how the World resolves interaction when it never stops being true.

The answer presented is Mutual Recognition: a Recognition-based mode of interaction that produces effects that act like magic without actually using magic.
